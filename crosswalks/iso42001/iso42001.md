## 1. Scope of ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 specifies requirements for an AI management system (AIMS): an organisation-level system for establishing policy, objectives, risk and impact assessment, and controls for the responsible development, provision or use of AI systems. It follows the harmonised management-system structure (clauses 4 to 10: context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement). Annex A lists reference control objectives and controls grouped as policies related to AI (A.2), internal organisation (A.3), resources for AI systems (A.4), assessing impacts of AI systems (A.5), AI system life cycle (A.6), data for AI systems (A.7), information for interested parties (A.8), use of AI systems (A.9) and third-party and customer relationships (A.10). Certification is to the management-system requirements; Annex A controls are selected through a statement of applicability.

## 2. Where it overlaps with AIDCF

- **Governance (GOV).** Clauses 5, 6, 9 and Annex A.2–A.3 overlap governance charter, role ownership, policy hierarchy, risk-based assurance planning and internal audit. All six GOV families are PARTIAL.
- **Resources for AI systems (A.4).** A.4 expects the organisation to document resources for AI systems, including system and computing resources. This is the closest 42001 comes to AI infrastructure and gives a documented inventory that can seed CAP-01 and CMP-01.
- **Third parties (A.10).** Overlaps external provider dependency (DEP-03) and supplier due diligence (SUP-01) at objective level.
- **Use of AI systems (A.9) and life cycle (A.6).** Overlap SEC-06 where AI agents or automation take action on infrastructure: 42001 expects responsible-use objectives and human oversight; AIDCF SEC-06 requires bounded authority, approvals, command allowlisting and safe rollback.
- **Evidence and assessment (EVA).** Documented information (7.5), internal audit (9.2), corrective action (10.2) and continual improvement (10.1) overlap EVA-01, EVA-03, EVA-04 and EVA-06.

## 3. Where AIDCF goes beyond ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 governs the AI system and the organisation's management of it. It treats infrastructure as a resource and does not assess whether that resource can sustain the AI system's outcomes. AIDCF adds:

- Capability Instances with Service Outcomes and tolerances (availability, performance, integrity, recovery) across declared operating states, rather than AI objectives.
- Physical, power, thermal, environmental, compute, storage, network and operations domains (PHY, POW, THM, ENV, CMP, STO, NET, OPS), which have no 42001 counterpart.
- Dependency, common-mode and concentration analysis (DEP-02, DEP-04) and fault-domain and recovery design (RES).
- Claim types, evidence quality and sufficiency floors, UNKNOWN Records and Critical Gates (AIDCF-008, AIDCF-010), none of which exist in a management-system audit.

## 4. Using ISO/IEC 42001 outputs as AIDCF evidence

| Output | AIDCF evidence class | Useful for | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| AIMS certificate and certification-body audit report | E5 Independent and third-party | GOV-01 to GOV-04, EVA-03 (existence of governance processes) | Scope statement may exclude infrastructure; certificate asserts management-system conformity, not infrastructure capability |
| AI policy, roles, statement of applicability | E1 Documentary | GOV-02, GOV-03 | Design-level only (DESIGN claim) |
| A.4 resource documentation | E1/E2 | CAP-01, CMP-01, CMP-03 | Inventory accuracy must be reconciled to as-built configuration (CG-02) |
| AI system impact assessments (6.1.4, 8.4) | E1/E7 | CAP-03 stakeholder consequence | Does not set infrastructure impact tolerance |
| Internal audit results and nonconformities | E5 (if independent) / E1 | EVA-03, EVA-04 | Nonconformities map to Findings; absence of nonconformity is not evidence of infrastructure effectiveness |

Evaluate each under AIDCF-008, in particular the scope statement of the AIMS (clause 4.3) against the AIDCF assessment boundary.

## 5. Gaps and cautions

- An AIMS scope that names the AI service but excludes the hosting facility or third-party colocation does not provide evidence for any infrastructure family.
- Annex A control identifiers below group level (for example A.4.5) are cited only in notes where the author is confident; reviewers should confirm against the licensed text.
- ISO/IEC 42001 relies on the organisation's information security management for access control and security operations; use the [ISO/IEC 27001 crosswalk](../iso27001/iso27001.md) for SEC families.
