## 1 Purpose and scope

This guide explains how to apply the 24 THM controls when assessing whether a Capability Instance can continue to remove the heat generated by its AI infrastructure across the declared operating states. It is written for assessors, facility and thermal engineering authorities, and AI platform operators who need a shared view of what "thermal continuity" means for accelerator-dense facilities and what evidence demonstrates it.

The scope is the complete heat-removal path, from the heat source at the accelerator package to the final heat sink:

- **Air-side systems**: computer-room air handlers (CRAH), fan walls, in-row coolers, rear-door heat exchangers, hot- or cold-aisle containment and airflow management, including the residual air-side heat from liquid-cooled racks.
- **Liquid systems**: direct-to-chip cold plates, rack manifolds and quick-disconnect couplings, the technology cooling system (TCS, the secondary loop serving IT), coolant distribution units (CDUs, either liquid-to-liquid or liquid-to-air), immersion tanks and their heat exchangers.
- **Facility water and heat rejection**: the facility water system (FWS), chillers, dry coolers, adiabatic coolers, cooling towers and the condenser water system, thermal storage, make-up water and any heat-reuse or district-energy interface.
- **Fluids**: water, water-glycol solutions, dielectric immersion fluids and refrigerants, their chemistry, filtration and hazards.
- **Controls and instrumentation**: CDU controllers, BMS sequences, chiller plant managers, sensors and leak detection, as they affect heat removal.

Out of scope here (but linked through §14): electrical supply to mechanical plant (POW), environmental performance metrics such as PUE and WUE (ENV), fire detection and suppression (PHY), cyber security of controllers (SEC), and the accelerator's own thermal management and throttling behaviour (CMP).

## 2 Why this domain matters for AI capability

Accelerator-dense AI infrastructure changes the thermal problem in four ways that make conventional data-centre assumptions unsafe.

**Density and liquid dependence.** Racks for current training platforms commonly draw tens to well over a hundred kilowatts, most of which is captured by direct-to-chip liquid cooling. Air alone cannot remove this heat. The heat-removal path therefore includes pumps, heat exchangers, valves and fluid inventory that have no equivalent in an air-cooled hall, and each is a potential single point of failure.

**Low thermal mass and short ride-through.** A cold plate and its local coolant hold very little heat. When coolant flow stops, accelerator temperatures rise within seconds, and devices throttle or trip to protect themselves. The margin between "pumps stop" and "capability lost" is therefore a matter of seconds to minutes, not the tens of minutes an air-cooled room with large air volume might tolerate. Thermal ride-through during utility loss, UPS transfer, generator start and chiller restart is a first-order design and assurance concern (MCF-THM-003).

**Synchronous load transients.** Large training jobs run thousands of accelerators in lock-step. Compute phases and communication or checkpoint phases alternate, and job start, stop or failure moves the whole cluster's power and heat together. Cooling controls tuned for slowly varying enterprise loads can oscillate or lag when hundreds of kilowatts step simultaneously (MCF-THM-021).

**Concentration of capability.** A single CDU group or FWS branch may serve the scale-up domains on which an entire training run depends. A cooling fault that affects one fault domain can halt a job across the cluster, because synchronous training generally stops when any participating domain is lost. Thermal fault domains must therefore be understood in terms of the Service Outcomes they carry, not only the kilowatts they remove.

The consequence for assurance is that a thermal claim must cover the whole path, the transients and the degraded states. Steady-state capacity at mild ambient on a lightly loaded hall says little about continuity under full synchronous load on the hottest day during a power event. AIDCF-008 §49 states the same limit: no generalisation from steady low load to full transient AI demand.

## 3 Reference patterns and variants

AIDCF-013 provides the generic system cards for air and liquid cooling. The patterns below are the variants an assessor is most likely to meet; most facilities combine several.

| Pattern | Description | Characteristic assurance concerns |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Air-cooled with containment | CRAH or fan walls supplying contained cold aisles or extracting from contained hot aisles; chilled water from chillers with or without economiser | Inlet temperature compliance at rack height; bypass and recirculation; CRAH redundancy at actual chilled-water temperature; containment–fire interface |
| P2 Rear-door heat exchanger | Passive or active water-cooled doors on each rack, fed from the FWS or a dedicated loop | Water in the IT row; door fan power; leak detection at each door; capacity at actual water temperature |
| P3 Direct-to-chip with liquid-to-liquid CDU | Cold plates on accelerators (and often CPUs, memory, NICs) on a TCS loop; CDUs exchange heat to the FWS | CDU pump redundancy; approach temperature; W-class compatibility; TCS integrity; quick-disconnect management; residual heat to air |
| P4 Direct-to-chip with liquid-to-air CDU | CDU rejects TCS heat to hall air through a coil; used where no FWS is available, typically in retrofits | Air-side capacity must absorb the full liquid load; hall temperature drives CDU capacity; air and liquid paths become one fault domain |
| P5 Single-phase immersion | IT submerged in dielectric fluid in tanks; fluid circulated to a heat exchanger on the FWS | Fluid compatibility with components; fluid degradation; handling hazards; tank leak containment; service procedures |
| P6 Chiller-less high-temperature water | FWS at W32 or above, rejected through dry coolers or adiabatic coolers without mechanical refrigeration | Capacity at extreme ambient; adiabatic water dependency and legionella control; no chiller restart delay but full ambient exposure |
| P7 Chilled-water with chillers and thermal storage | Mechanical refrigeration with chilled-water storage to bridge restart | Chiller restart-to-capacity; storage sizing for ride-through; refrigerant safety |
| P8 Heat reuse or district interface | Heat exported to a district network or adjacent consumer | Exported heat path must not be the only heat sink; fall-back rejection required |

### 3.1 Technology cooling system versus facility water system

The distinction between the TCS and the FWS is central to direct-to-chip assurance. In the ASHRAE TC 9.9 liquid-cooling terminology, the **technology cooling system** is the loop that contacts IT equipment (cold plates, manifolds, couplings) and must meet the IT OEM's requirements for fluid, cleanliness, temperature, flow and pressure. The **facility water system** carries heat from CDUs to heat rejection and is designed to facility standards. The CDU is the boundary between them. Most IT-side failure modes (fouling, leaks at couplings, flow imbalance) arise in the TCS; most capacity and ride-through failure modes arise in the FWS and heat rejection. Assessors should therefore expect two sets of evidence, two owners (frequently the AI platform operator or OEM for the TCS and the facility operator for the FWS) and a documented interface contract between them (AIDCF-013 fluid and thermal interface contracts).

### 3.2 Liquid-cooling classes

ASHRAE TC 9.9 classifies facility water supplied to liquid-cooled IT by the maximum facility water supply temperature at the interface: classes W17, W27, W32, W40, W45 and W+ (above 45 °C). The class determines whether mechanical refrigeration is needed at the site's climate, how much approach temperature is available across the CDU, and whether heat reuse is practical. The design basis (MCF-THM-009) should state the class adopted and show that, at the worst-case facility water supply temperature for that class plus the CDU approach temperature, the TCS supply temperature remains within the accelerator OEM limit. Air-side environments are classified separately; the ASHRAE TC 9.9 recommended envelope for classes A1–A4 is 18–27 °C dry-bulb at the IT inlet, and later editions added a high-density air class with a narrower envelope. Site-specific OEM limits take precedence where they are tighter.

The THM domain implements the following Required Functions (AIDCF-014 class RequiredFunction). Each is linked to Service Outcomes through the canonical chain (Capability Instance → Service Outcome → Required Function → InfrastructureSystem).

| RF-THM-2 Transport heat | Move coolant and air through TCS, CDUs and FWS at required flow and pressure | MCF-THM-007, -010, -011 |
| RF-THM-3 Reject heat | Transfer heat to ambient or another sink at the design ambient | MCF-THM-004, -013, -014, -016 |
| RF-THM-4 Maintain fluid fitness | Keep coolant and water chemically, physically and microbiologically within limits | MCF-THM-017, -018 |
| RF-THM-5 Detect and contain leaks | Detect, locate, isolate and drain liquid releases within their fault domain | MCF-THM-019, -020 |
| RF-THM-6 Control and sense | Keep temperatures and pressures stable under transient load and fail safe on controller or sensor loss | MCF-THM-021, -022 |
| RF-THM-7 Sustain through states | Maintain heat removal through component loss, maintenance and power events | MCF-THM-002, -003, -015, -023 |
| RF-THM-8 Recover | Restore heat removal and readmit IT load in a controlled sequence | MCF-THM-024 |

The linkage to capability is quantitative: the heat-load model (MCF-THM-001) converts the Capability Instance's compute deployment into heat per fault domain; capacity controls test that heat against what each stage can remove; and usable compute capacity (MCF-CMP-009) reports the accelerators that are throttled because the thermal function is not fully delivered. A thermal shortfall therefore appears in two places: as a THM control result and as a reduction in usable accelerator capacity. Assessors should reconcile the two.

## 5 Dependencies, fault domains, common-mode concerns

### 5.1 Dependencies

The heat-removal path depends on:

- **Electrical supply** to pumps, CDUs, fans, chillers and controls. The critical question is which of these are on UPS and which only on generator-backed supply (POW-03, POW-05).
- **Water supply** for evaporative and adiabatic heat rejection and for loop make-up (MCF-THM-015; ENV-03).
- **Controls networks and BMS** for sequencing and setpoints (MCF-THM-021; SEC-02).
- **Ambient conditions** at the heat-rejection plant, including local recirculation (MCF-THM-004, -014).
- **OEM requirements and support** for TCS fluid, cold-plate design and CDU firmware (DEP-03, SUP-04).
- **Fluid and chemical supply chains** for glycol, inhibitors, immersion fluid and refrigerant (SUP-05, ENV-05).
- **Third-party plant** in colocation or shared campuses (MCF-THM-016; DEP-05).

### 5.2 Fault domains

A thermal fault domain is the set of IT equipment that loses cooling together when a single element fails. Typical nesting is: cold plate → server tray → rack manifold → CDU (or CDU group) → TCS loop → FWS branch or header → chiller or dry-cooler train → central plant. Air-side fault domains (CRAH zone, containment pod) overlap liquid fault domains and do not usually align with them. The assessor should obtain or construct the fault-domain table that maps each rack to every thermal element it depends on (MCF-THM-001 requires this apportionment) and then compare it with the compute fault-domain mapping (MCF-CMP-008).

### 5.3 Common-mode concerns

Redundancy in the THM domain is frequently defeated by elements that are shared across nominally independent trains. Common modes the assessor should look for explicitly include:

- a single FWS header or a single pair of headers with a common isolation valve;
- one controller or PLC sequencing all CDUs or all chillers, with no independent fail-safe;
- CDU pumps for redundant groups fed from the same UPS output or distribution board;
- a shared make-up water supply, water-treatment dosing system or expansion vessel;
- a single BMS server or network switch on which all cooling setpoints depend;
- identical CDU firmware deployed to all units at once (a software common mode analogous to MCF-CMP-015);
- a coolant batch contaminated at source and used to fill all loops;
- heat-rejection plant co-located so that one recirculation plume or one roof fire affects all trains;
- a single external heat sink (district network) without independent fall-back.

Each identified common mode should be recorded with its treatment and owner (MCF-THM-016) and, where it affects the outcome, raised to CG-03 and CG-09 evaluation.

## 7 Safeguards

Safeguards are organised by the AIDCF-004 functions prevent, detect, contain, correct and recover.

- **Prevent**: capacity with margin at design conditions (MCF-THM-002, -004, -007, -010, -013); redundancy and ride-through (MCF-THM-003); airflow and containment discipline (MCF-THM-005, -006); compatible fluids and chemistry (MCF-THM-017, -018); controlled coupling procedures (MCF-THM-012); stable controls (MCF-THM-021); planned maintenance with stated residual redundancy (MCF-THM-023).
- **Detect**: inlet air monitoring (MCF-THM-008); TCS pressure, flow, ΔT and make-up monitoring (MCF-THM-011); leak detection with location (MCF-THM-019); sensor plausibility (MCF-THM-022); accelerator throttle telemetry as a lagging indicator (MCF-CMP-017).
- **Contain**: isolation valves, drip trays and drainage sized to the fault domain (MCF-THM-020); fault-domain spread of compute so that one thermal domain does not halt the outcome (MCF-CMP-008).
- **Correct**: automatic pump failover (MCF-THM-010); high-ambient and load-management procedures (MCF-THM-004); corrective water treatment (MCF-THM-018).
- **Recover**: thermal recovery sequence and staged readmission (MCF-THM-024), aligned with power restoration (POW-06) and compute re-qualification (MCF-CMP-023).

A safeguard is credited only for the states in which it is evidenced. For example, a CDU's N+1 pump redundancy credited in NORMAL is not automatically credited in MAINTENANCE when one pump is out for service.

Notes on the mapping:

- **CG-06 Thermal continuity** has seven CRITICAL THM controls covering the minimum argument that the heat-removal path is sufficient across states: as-built capacity (MCF-THM-002), redundancy and ride-through (-003), capacity at design ambient (-004), CDU capacity and pump failover (-010), TCS integrity (-011), chiller capacity where chillers exist (-013) and water continuity where heat rejection depends on water (-015). The CG-06 evidence floor in AIDCF-008 Part XIII is S5 with classes E1+E2+E3+E4. Accordingly the CRITICAL CG-06 controls carry profile tier CP-5 and sufficiency floor S5.

Practical expectations by claim type:

- **DESIGN (AL2)**: design basis documents, P&IDs, OEM requirement documents, dependency maps and calculations. Evidence is E1 with E7 analysis. A DESIGN result says nothing about the as-built state; MCF-THM-001, -009, -016 and -017 are DESIGN claims and support, but do not substitute for, the capacity and integrity controls.
- **IMPLEMENTATION (AL3)**: as-built reconciliation and commissioning. Evidence is E1 plus E2 configuration exports (BMS point lists, CDU configuration, unit schedules) and E3 inspection, with E4 commissioning records where capacity is claimed. MCF-THM-002 is an IMPLEMENTATION claim but is CG-06 CRITICAL and therefore requires E4 load-bank commissioning at S5.
- **OPERATION (AL4)**: telemetry and records over a representative period that includes the relevant seasons and states. MCF-THM-004 requires a period including the peak-ambient season; MCF-THM-018 requires twelve months of water-quality results. Telemetry is only as good as its sensors (MCF-THM-022) and its completeness; gaps shall be reported.
- **EFFECTIVENESS (AL5)**: tests under defined adverse conditions — pump failover, chiller restart, leak isolation, step-load control response and leak-detection wet tests. E4 test records shall include authorisation, initial state, method, expected result, observed result and anomalies (AIDCF-008 E4).
- **RESILIENCE (AL5)**: integrated or scenario evidence across states — black-building or utility-loss tests with time-stamped coolant traces (MCF-THM-003), water switchover (MCF-THM-015) and recovery exercises (MCF-THM-024).

Evidence quality for thermal claims is most often limited by three issues: telemetry from uncalibrated or poorly placed sensors (reliability, R), tests performed at partial load or mild ambient (coverage, C, and freshness against current load), and commissioning records that predate densification (freshness, F). The assessor should rate these dimensions explicitly rather than accept a single "tested" statement.

**DG-THM-REQ-003** Evidence of capacity or ride-through obtained at a heat load lower than the current admitted load shall not support a CONFORMING result for the current load unless extrapolation is performed by a documented method and reviewed independently.

**DG-THM-REQ-004** Thermal telemetry used as E3 evidence for an OPERATION, EFFECTIVENESS or RESILIENCE claim shall be accompanied by evidence of sensor calibration status and data completeness for the period.

## 11 Assessment guidance

### 11.1 Sequencing the assessment

1. **Fix the demand.** Start with MCF-THM-001. Without a current heat-load model apportioned to fault domains, every capacity control lacks an acceptance figure. Reconcile the model to the IT power budget and to compute deployment records (MCF-CMP-001, -003).
2. **Trace the path.** Using the P&ID, trace each fault domain from rack to final heat sink and list each stage. Build the stage-by-stage capacity table required by MCF-THM-002 and find the weakest stage. In direct-to-chip halls the weakest stage is often the CDU group, the FWS pumps or the dry coolers at design ambient, rather than the chillers.
3. **Test the states.** For each CRITICAL CG-06 control, check which operating states the evidence covers: NORMAL, MAINTENANCE (one unit out), DEGRADED (one unit failed), EMERGENCY (utility loss, extreme ambient) and RECOVERY. Record uncovered states as U5 coverage-gap UNKNOWN Records.
4. **Look for common modes.** Walk the plant with the dependency map (MCF-THM-016) and verify shared headers, controllers, power boards and water supplies physically.
5. **Check fluids and leaks.** Review chemistry and microbiological results, coupling management and leak detection coverage against the room layout.
6. **Close with controls and recovery.** Review fail-safe evidence and recovery exercises last, because they depend on understanding the plant.

### 11.2 Sampling

Sampling follows AIDCF-009. For THM the population is usually the set of fault domains, not individual sensors. Assessors should include, at minimum, the fault domain carrying the highest heat load, the fault domain serving the scale-up domains of the largest training job, and any fault domain modified since the last commissioning. For sensor and leak-detection controls, sample by zone so that every wetted zone type (CDU skid, rack manifold, rear door, under-floor or overhead pipework, immersion tank) is represented.

### 11.3 Interpreting results

- A capacity control with commissioning evidence that predates densification is **UNKNOWN** for the current load, not CONFORMING.
- A ride-through analysis without a transient test is sufficient for a DESIGN view but leaves MCF-THM-003 **UNKNOWN** at its RESILIENCE claim.
- A margin below the profile's required margin, with capacity above load, is **PARTIAL**, which under the AIDCF-010 roll-up rule yields CG-06 FAIL if the control is CRITICAL. The assessor should record the margin shortfall as a Finding and identify the load limit that would restore conformity.
- Throttling events in accelerator telemetry (MCF-CMP-017) during the period are evidence that challenges a CONFORMING thermal claim and shall be reconciled before the claim is accepted.

**DG-THM-REQ-005** Where accelerator telemetry shows thermal throttling in the assessment period, the assessor shall record the events as challenging evidence against the relevant THM controls and shall resolve or bound the conflict before concluding those controls are CONFORMING.

**DG-THM-REQ-006** The assessor shall record for each CG-06 CRITICAL control the operating states covered by the evidence, and shall record each declared operating state not covered as an UNKNOWN Record with its severity.

## 12 Testing safety

Thermal tests are among the most hazardous and capability-affecting activities in an AI facility. Pump failover, chiller restart, utility-loss and isolation tests can trip production IT within seconds, and pressure tests, refrigerant work and evaporative-plant sampling present direct hazards to people.

- **Authority.** Hazardous or capability-affecting tests shall be performed only under a permit-to-work and method statement approved by the site authorising engineer, with the AI platform operator's agreement on the load at risk (DG-THM-REQ-007). Assessors observe, review and, where authorised, witness; they do not operate plant.
- **Preference for existing evidence.** Before requesting a new disruptive test, the assessor should seek evidence from previous commissioning, scheduled tests and real events with full telemetry.
- **Abort criteria.** Live thermal tests shall define abort criteria based on TCS supply temperature, IT inlet temperature or accelerator temperature, and a person with authority to abort shall be present (DG-THM-REQ-008).
- **Pressure systems.** Pressure tests of TCS or FWS sections require pressure-equipment competence, isolation from IT and exclusion zones.
- **Biological and chemical hazards.** Sampling of evaporative systems requires respiratory protection according to the legionella risk assessment; coolant and immersion fluid handling follows the safety data sheet.
- **Refrigerants.** Only certified refrigeration personnel work on refrigerant circuits; machinery-room entry follows the refrigerant detection and ventilation status.
- **Electrical proximity.** Wet tests of leak detection use minimal water away from energised equipment.

**DG-THM-REQ-007** A THM test that can interrupt heat removal from production IT, release fluid or pressure, or expose people to refrigerant, chemical or biological hazards shall not be performed or requested by an assessor without a permit-to-work and method statement approved by the site authorising engineer and the agreement of the AI platform operator.

**DG-THM-REQ-008** Every live thermal test shall define abort criteria and name the person authorised to abort before the test begins.

## 17 Worked synthetic example

**Synthetic example. All names, figures and results below are invented for illustration and do not describe any real facility, design or assessment.**

**Context.** Capability Instance CI-SYN-THM-01 is a 2,048-accelerator training cluster in Hall B of a synthetic facility. The Service Outcome is sustained training throughput with interruption not exceeding the job's checkpoint-restart tolerance. The cluster comprises 32 racks of 64 accelerators. The capability profile adopts a rack design heat load of 120 kW, a liquid-capture ratio of 80 %, a design capacity margin of 10 % and N+1 CDU redundancy. Pattern P3 (direct-to-chip, liquid-to-liquid CDUs) is combined with P6 (W32 facility water, dry coolers with adiabatic trim, no chillers).

**Demand (MCF-THM-001).** Total IT heat is 32 × 120 kW = 3,840 kW, of which 3,072 kW goes to liquid and 768 kW to air. Racks are served by four CDU groups of eight racks, so each group carries 8 × 120 kW × 0.8 = 768 kW of liquid heat. The model, however, takes the 80 % capture ratio from OEM data stated at a TCS supply temperature lower than the site's design value, and the OEM data give a lower ratio at the site's temperature; the model owner had not updated the figure. The assessor records a Finding and a U1 UNKNOWN for the residual air heat, US3, pending OEM confirmation.

**Capacity (MCF-THM-002, -010).** Each CDU group has three CDUs rated 450 kW at the design FWS supply temperature; with N+1, demonstrated group capacity is 2 × 450 kW = 900 kW against 768 kW, a margin of 17 %, which meets the 10 % profile margin. Commissioning load-bank records exist for all four groups. Pump failover tests were performed at commissioning when only 16 racks were installed; the current load is double. MCF-THM-010 (EFFECTIVENESS) is therefore **UNKNOWN** for current load (U5, US4).

**Ride-through (MCF-THM-003).** CDU pumps and controls are on UPS; dry-cooler fans are on generator-backed supply only. The ride-through calculation uses a combined TCS and FWS inventory of about 12 m³ of water-glycol (heat capacity roughly 47 MJ/K): at 3,072 kW of liquid heat the bulk coolant temperature rises at about 0.065 K/s while heat rejection is lost, reaching the 5 K transient limit in roughly 75 s. Generator start and fan restart are designed to complete within 30 s. The calculation is credible, but no integrated utility-loss test has been performed since densification. MCF-THM-003 is **UNKNOWN** (U5, US4).

**Ambient (MCF-THM-004).** The design ambient basis is documented from a named climatic dataset with a 2 K climate allowance. The facility has operated for eight months, none in the peak season. MCF-THM-004 is **UNKNOWN** (U5, US4) for EMERGENCY and peak-season states.

**Water (MCF-THM-015, -018).** Adiabatic trim depends on mains water with 24 h of storage against a profile requirement of 48 h. Capacity is above load but autonomy is below requirement: MCF-THM-015 is **PARTIAL**. The legionella risk assessment covers the adiabatic pads with monthly microbiological sampling, all within limits: MCF-THM-018 is **CONFORMING**.

**Not applicable.** MCF-THM-013 (Chiller Capacity) is recorded NOT_APPLICABLE with approved rationale: the site has no mechanical refrigeration, and dry-cooler capacity at design ambient is covered by MCF-THM-004.

**Gate Result.** Under the AIDCF-010 roll-up rule, CG-06 is **FAIL** because a CRITICAL control (MCF-THM-015) is PARTIAL. Had MCF-THM-015 been CONFORMING, CG-06 would still have been UNKNOWN because MCF-THM-003, -004 and -010 are UNKNOWN. CG-04 has no failing THM input from this example. The decision consequence is determined by the CG-06 ceiling in AIDCF-010.

**Actions recorded.** (1) Increase water storage or agree a tested tanker supply to reach 48 h autonomy, or adopt a profile tolerance with approved load reduction in water-restricted states; (2) repeat CDU pump failover tests at current load in a planned window; (3) perform an integrated utility-loss test with time-stamped coolant traces, or a calibrated simulation reviewed independently; (4) obtain OEM liquid-capture data at site TCS temperature and update the heat-load model; (5) reassess MCF-THM-004 after the first peak season. Each action carries a reassessment trigger for the affected controls.
