## 1. What changes and why

v1.0 §1 says the back-test does not show that AIDCF does better than any other method. v1.1 adds an exploratory paired comparison: for the same knowable pre-event causal factors, do AIDCF and each conventional review method surface them? The comparison is on paper, against each method's written criteria. It is designed so that AIDCF can come out ahead, behind or inconclusive, and each of those outcomes is reported on the same footing.

## 2. Comparator methods and the frozen specification

Each method is declared before coding in a new file, `baselines.csv`, with: `baseline_id`; name; the selected standards or procedures with **edition and assessment profile**; typical scope; normal deliverable; evidence the reviewer normally examines; realistic constraints (duration, sampling, access); numbered legitimate exclusions (`EXC-…`); and numbered applicability rules (`APP-…`). A practitioner of the method who is not the framework author checks each row. The file is versioned, approved and frozen with a SHA-256 hash in the run log before any coding. Reviewers may cite only requirements in the frozen editions and profiles; citing a stronger requirement from elsewhere is not allowed.

| ID | Method | Main references (edition fixed in `baselines.csv`) |
|---|---|---|
| BL-TIER | Topology certification or classification | Uptime Tier, ANSI/TIA-942, EN 50600 / ISO/IEC 22237 |
| BL-CX | Commissioning and integrated systems testing | ASHRAE Guideline 0 / 1.1, Level 1–5 Cx practice |
| BL-ENG | Engineering and reliability studies | Power system studies (IEEE 3000 series), FMEA / FMECA (IEC 60812), FTA (IEC 61025), cooling and CFD studies |
| BL-OT | OT security risk assessment | IEC 62443-3-2 and 3-3 |
| BL-ISMS | Information security management audit | ISO/IEC 27001 with 27002, SOC 2 |
| BL-TDD | Technical due diligence | Practice-based; scope from a declared reference engagement letter (lowest confidence, reported as such) |

BL-ENG is a single method, so it casts one vote in BL-ANY. Each BL-ENG judgement records `eng_discipline` (POWER, COOLING, OTHER) and `eng_technique` (for example FMEA, FTA, LOAD_FLOW, CFD), and BL-ENG results are also reported by discipline.

ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF are not used as baselines, because they do not claim to assess physical infrastructure.

**BL-ANY, the combined-method coverage benchmark.** BL-ANY is the union of the six methods (§5.2). It is a demanding benchmark, not a typical conventional assessment. Few facilities commission all six, it does not account for cost or effort, and it is never one review. Every output labels it this way and reports the six methods individually alongside it.

## 3. Judgements

### 3.1 One judgement vocabulary for both sides (P0-02)

Baseline judgements use the same scale as AIDCF judgements (v1.0 §10), plus two values that apply only to baselines. This removes any translation between scales.

| Value | Meaning | AIDCF | Baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| DETECTED | The method's criteria and normal evidence would surface the condition (v1.0 tests D1–D4, read against the method's frozen text) | Yes | Yes |
| PARTIALLY_DETECTED | Covered, but surfaced only in part or with its consequence understated | Yes | Yes |
| NOT_DETECTED | The method addresses the condition, but its criteria, read literally, would be met despite it | Yes | Yes |
| NOT_ADDRESSED | No criterion of the method covers the condition | Yes | Yes |
| INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION | The source or the method's text cannot settle the judgement | Yes | Yes |
| OUT_OF_SCOPE | The method applies, but the condition falls under a declared `EXC-…` | No | Yes, cite the `EXC-…` |
| NOT_APPLICABLE | A declared `APP-…` rule says the method does not apply to this factor | No | Yes, cite the `APP-…` |

DETECTED, PARTIALLY_DETECTED, NOT_DETECTED, NOT_ADDRESSED and OUT_OF_SCOPE are **determinate**. INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION is **indeterminate**. NOT_APPLICABLE takes the method out of the comparison for that factor. Every determinate judgement cites the clause or procedure step relied on (BT-REQ-010 extended to baselines).

### 3.2 Criterion coverage and estimated operational detectability (P0-03)

- **Criterion coverage** is the §3.1 judgement under the v1.0 §4 assumptions (competent reviewer, full access, no outcome knowledge). It is the primary comparative measure, and it is coded for every eligible factor and method.
- **Estimated operational detectability (EOD)** is a reviewer's estimate of whether a competent practitioner, applying the method under the realistic constraints declared in `baselines.csv` (and, for AIDCF, in an AIDCF row taken from AIDCF-009 sampling and duration), would reasonably have surfaced the factor. It is an expert estimate, not observed performance, and every output says so.
  - Values: LIKELY, POSSIBLE, UNLIKELY, INDETERMINATE.
  - Each value records `eod_assumptions` (the constraints and evidence assumed) and `eod_confidence` (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH).
  - INDETERMINATE is used whenever the information cannot support a credible estimate.
  - **Sample.** EOD is coded only on a prespecified sample: before coding, the adjudicator draws 30% of eligible factors (and at least one per incident), stratified by `failure_category` with a recorded seed. Both sides code EOD for the same sampled factors.
  - EOD results are reported in separate tables, never combined with criterion coverage, and never described as detection rates.

## 4. Information boundary, blinding and reviewer independence

- Baseline coders receive the same PRE and EVENT dossier as AIDCF coders. After their own factor list is locked (§4A), they receive the consolidated factor list. Each factor is given as its `causal_factor` text only, without `cause_family`, `scenario_family`, `library_scenario` or any AIDCF control.
- Baseline coders do not see any AIDCF judgement until their file is locked by SHA-256 hash in the run log. AIDCF coders do not see baseline judgements until theirs are locked.
- Only factors with `knowability` STATED_KNOWN or DISCOVERABLE, identified before the reveal, enter the comparison.
- Baseline coders and the adjudicator are not the framework author and have no role in AIDCF's development. Each declares prior involvement with AIDCF and their `prior_familiarity` with the incident, and both are reported.
- **Coder counts.** The target is two independent coders per method for the primary comparative sample, reconciled as in v1.0 §13. If that is not feasible for a method, a single coder codes all of it and a second independent coder codes a prespecified representative subset of at least 20%, with κ reported. If only one qualified practitioner is available for a method, its comparative results are labelled exploratory and the limitation is disclosed. Every output reports the number of distinct independent reviewers per method, separately from the number of coding records.

## 4A. Factor discovery, provenance and adjudication

- Both sides discover factors from the same dossier before the reveal. AIDCF coders list factors as in v1.0 §8. Each baseline coder independently lists any further pre-event factors their method would have raised, and locks that list by hash before seeing the AIDCF list. Neither side sees the other's interpretations before its list is locked.
- The adjudicator merges the lists into one consolidated factor list. Each factor records `factor_origin` (AIDCF, BASELINE, BOTH or ADJUDICATOR) and `factor_source` (the coder IDs and dossier passages it came from). Merges follow recorded rules. A factor is dropped only with a recorded reason, and dropped factors are listed in the report.
- Both sides judge every consolidated factor.
- Every metric is reported for all factors, for AIDCF-origin factors, and for BASELINE-origin factors, together with the distribution of factors by origin.

## 5. Paired analysis

### 5.1 Normative mapping (P0-02)

Readings: **strict** counts DETECTED as surfaced. **Inclusive** counts DETECTED or PARTIALLY_DETECTED as surfaced.

| Judgement (either side) | Strict | Inclusive | Miss subtype recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| DETECTED | Surfaced | Surfaced | — |
| PARTIALLY_DETECTED | Miss | Surfaced | PARTIAL (strict only) |
| NOT_DETECTED | Miss | Miss | ADDRESSED_NOT_DETECTED |
| NOT_ADDRESSED | Miss | Miss | NOT_ADDRESSED |
| OUT_OF_SCOPE (baseline) | Miss | Miss | DECLARED_EXCLUSION |
| INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION | Excluded | Excluded | — (reason: INDETERMINATE_AIDCF or INDETERMINATE_BASELINE) |
| NOT_APPLICABLE (baseline) | Excluded for this method | Excluded for this method | — (reason: NOT_APPLICABLE) |

For a single method, a factor's pair is **eligible** when both sides map to Surfaced or Miss. Its class is a (both surface), b (AIDCF only), c (method only) or d (neither). Every table also breaks down b, c and d by miss subtype, so that "not addressed", "addressed but not detected", "partly covered" and "declared exclusion" stay distinguishable.

### 5.2 BL-ANY

For each factor, take the methods that are not NOT_APPLICABLE.

- BL-ANY is **Surfaced** if any of them maps to Surfaced.
- BL-ANY is a **Miss** only if every one of them maps to Miss.
- Otherwise (none surfaces it, and at least one is INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION) BL-ANY is **INDETERMINATE**, and the pair is excluded with reason INDETERMINATE_BASELINE.
- If every method is NOT_APPLICABLE, BL-ANY is NOT_APPLICABLE and the pair is excluded.

So AIDCF receives an AIDCF-only credit against BL-ANY only when its own judgement is DETECTED (strict) or DETECTED or PARTIALLY_DETECTED (inclusive), and every applicable method has a determinate judgement that maps to Miss.

### 5.3 Factor-level metric and denominators

- N(m, r) = a + b + c + d: the eligible pairs for method m (or BL-ANY) under reading r.
- **Net incremental coverage** = (b − c) / N(m, r). It ranges from −1 to +1.
- Every result shows a, b, c, d, N, and the excluded counts by reason, separately for each side.
- **Differential indeterminacy:** for each method, report the share of knowable factors that are INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION on the AIDCF side and on the method side, and the difference. If the difference exceeds 10 percentage points, the result carries a flag wherever it appears.

### 5.4 Incident-level analysis and sparse data (P0-04)

- **Common set.** Incident-level results for method m use only that incident's eligible factor pairs for m: the same factors for both sides. A factor excluded on either side is excluded for both, so neither side gains from the other's indeterminacy.
- **Incident eligibility.** An incident is eligible for method m if it has at least one eligible pair, and if no more than 50% of its knowable factors are excluded for m by INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION on either side. Incidents that fail the 50% rule are listed with their counts and are not used.
- **Incident classification.** Within the common set, an incident is surfaced by a side if any eligible factor is surfaced by that side, and is then classed a, b, c or d as at factor level.
- **Missingness profile.** Every incident-level table reports eligible incidents, incidents excluded by each rule, and the per-side indeterminacy shares.
- **Intervals.** 95% intervals come from an incident-level cluster bootstrap: 10,000 resamples, with the seed recorded in the run log. If a method has fewer than 10 eligible incidents, no interval is reported, only counts. A stratum with fewer than 5 eligible incidents is reported descriptively. A table with no eligible pairs says so, and no metric is computed. A narrow interval is not described as evidence of validity, because systematic coding bias is not captured by resampling.

### 5.6 Headline rule

No single figure is the headline. The headline for each method and for BL-ANY is the full strict table (a, b, c, d, excluded by reason), followed by the inclusive table, then net incremental coverage with its interval. No hypothesis test, no pass mark.

## 6. Baseline-only results and gap disposition (P0-01)

- Every baseline-only pair (class c) is a **comparative disadvantage** for AIDCF. It is listed in the main report by incident, factor, method, clause cited and miss subtype, and it counts in every table as a c. Nothing in this section changes any paired classification.
- Separately, the adjudicator gives each c result a **gap disposition**, with a written rationale and the AIDCF text relied on:

| Disposition | Meaning | Required citation |
|---|---|---|
| ACTIONABLE_GAP | AIDCF should cover this; recorded as G1–G3 under v1.0 §12 and fed back as a change proposal | The gap record |
| OUTSIDE_DECLARED_BOUNDARY | The factor is outside the assurance boundary that AIDCF declares | AIDCF clause defining the boundary |
| BELOW_INTENDED_GRANULARITY | AIDCF deliberately works at a coarser level | AIDCF clause stating the granularity |
| DELEGATED_TO_STANDARD | AIDCF requires another named standard that would cover it | The AIDCF control or clause that requires that standard |
| DISPUTED | The adjudicator and either coder disagree | Both positions recorded |

- The rules for each disposition are fixed in the protocol before coding. A boundary, granularity or delegation disposition must cite text that existed in the frozen baseline (BT-REQ-009); arguments made after the results are seen are not allowed. The share of c results given each disposition is reported, and a high share of non-ACTIONABLE dispositions is itself reported as a finding about the framework's boundary.

## 7. Limits of the claim (new BT-REQ)

**BT-REQ-0xx** Comparative results are exploratory. Every output that reports them states that a paper-based retrospective comparison does not show higher incident prevention, better predictive validity, lower operational risk, better cost-effectiveness, or better detection by real engineering teams; that BL-ANY is a combined-method coverage benchmark, not one conventional assessment; and that estimated operational detectability is expert estimation, not observed performance.

## 10. Feasibility note for the reviewer

The design now asks for up to 12 independent practitioner coders (two for each of six methods), an adjudicator and AIDCF coders. With unpaid volunteers that is unlikely in a first run. The fallback in §4 (a single coder with a 20% double-coded subset, labelled exploratory) is therefore the realistic first outcome for most methods. A smaller first run, for example BL-ENG, BL-CX and BL-OT only, may produce more credible results than six thinly staffed methods. I would welcome a ruling on whether a staged first run is acceptable.
