# AIDCF-VAL-001 v1.1 draft: comparative validation (revision 3)

Draft for review, 2026-10-13. Not merged into `main`. Revision 2 answered KAI's six conditions and the instrument point. Revision 3 adds KAI's three further conditions: determinate-only BL-ANY credit (§5), symmetrical treatment of baseline-originated factors (§4A), and the combined-method label (§2). §9 maps each review question to its clause. No baseline coding starts until this is approved.

## 1. What changes and why

v1.0 §1 says the back-test does not show that AIDCF does better than any other method. v1.1 adds an exploratory paired comparison: for the same knowable pre-event causal factors, would AIDCF and each conventional review method have surfaced them? It answers one question only: on paper, at the level of each method's written criteria, does AIDCF surface factors that the methods a facility normally uses do not, and the reverse?

## 2. Comparator baselines (new §10A.1; KAI condition 1)

Baselines are fixed before coding in a new file `baselines.csv`. Each row declares: `baseline_id`, name, reference standards or procedures (with edition), typical scope, normal deliverable, evidence the reviewer normally examines, realistic constraints (duration, sampling, access), and numbered legitimate exclusions (`EXC-…`). Each row is drafted from public standards and checked, before coding, by a practitioner of that method who is not the framework author.

Proposed set. These are the methods a facility would realistically rely on, not the website's comparison list:

| ID | Method | Main references |
|---|---|---|
| BL-TIER | Topology certification or classification | Uptime Tier, ANSI/TIA-942, EN 50600 / ISO/IEC 22237 |
| BL-CX | Commissioning and integrated systems testing | ASHRAE Guideline 0 / 1.1, Level 1–5 Cx practice |
| BL-ENG | Engineering and reliability studies | Power system studies (IEEE 3000 series), FMEA / FMECA (IEC 60812), FTA (IEC 61025), cooling and CFD studies |
| BL-OT | OT security risk assessment | IEC 62443-3-2 and 3-3 |
| BL-ISMS | Information security management audit | ISO/IEC 27001 with 27002, SOC 2 |
| BL-TDD | Technical due diligence | Practice-based; scope from the engagement letter (lowest confidence, reported as such) |

ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF are not used as baselines. They do not claim to assess physical infrastructure, so including them would only add misses that mean nothing.

**BL-ANY, the combined-method coverage benchmark.** It is the union of the six methods: a factor counts as surfaced if any applicable method surfaces it. It is a demanding benchmark, not a typical conventional assessment. Few facilities commission all six, and never as one review. Every output labels it this way and reports the six methods separately alongside it.

**Applicability.** Each baseline row declares applicability rules (for example, BL-OT applies only where the incident's scope includes automation or control systems). For a given factor, a method that the rules make inapplicable is recorded NOT_APPLICABLE. That is not a miss: the method drops out of BL-ANY for that factor. Where a method applies but the factor falls under one of its declared exclusions, it is recorded OUT_OF_SCOPE, which counts as not surfaced.

## 3. Two outcomes, never combined (KAI condition 2)

Every judgement, for AIDCF and for each baseline, records two separate values:

- `criterion_coverage`: whether the method's written criteria and normal evidence would surface the factor, under the v1.0 §4 assumptions (competent reviewer, full access, no outcome knowledge). For AIDCF this is the existing factor-level `detection_judgement`.
- `operational_detectability`: whether a competent practitioner applying the method under the realistic constraints declared for it (in `baselines.csv` for baselines, and in a new AIDCF row in that file, taken from AIDCF-009 sampling and duration) would reasonably surface it.

Values for both: YES, PARTIAL, NO, OUT_OF_SCOPE (must cite a declared `EXC-…`; counts as not surfaced), NOT_APPLICABLE (baselines only, must cite a declared applicability rule; drops out), INDETERMINATE (the method's text or the source cannot settle it). YES, PARTIAL and NO are **determinate**. INDETERMINATE is not. Every YES, PARTIAL or NO cites the clause or procedure step relied on.

The two are reported as separate tables. No metric combines them.

## 4. Identical information boundary and blinding (KAI condition 4)

- Baseline coders receive the same PRE and EVENT dossier as AIDCF coders. After their own factor list is locked (§4A), they receive the consolidated factor list. Each factor is given as its `causal_factor` text only, without `cause_family`, `scenario_family`, `library_scenario` or any AIDCF control, so that AIDCF's framing is not passed on.
- Baseline coders do not see any AIDCF detection or gate judgement until their file is locked by SHA-256 hash in the run log.
- Only PRE_REVEAL factors with `knowability` STATED_KNOWN or DISCOVERABLE enter the comparison.
- Baseline coders are not the framework author, and at least one per method is a practitioner of that method. A second coder codes at least 20% of factors per method so that Cohen's κ can be reported.
- Reviewer independence: baseline coders and the adjudicator are not the framework author and have no role in AIDCF's development. Each declares any prior involvement with AIDCF and with the incident (`prior_familiarity`), and this is reported.

## 4A. Factor discovery and adjudication, symmetrical (KAI further condition 2)

- Factor discovery happens on both sides from the same PRE and EVENT dossier, before any reveal. AIDCF coders list factors as in v1.0 §8. Each baseline coder independently lists any further pre-event factors that their method would have raised, and that list is locked by hash before they see the AIDCF factor list.
- The independent adjudicator merges both lists into one consolidated factor list for the incident. Each factor gets `factor_origin`: AIDCF, BASELINE or BOTH. Duplicates are merged under recorded rules, and no factor is dropped without a recorded reason.
- Every consolidated factor is then judged by both sides: AIDCF coders judge baseline-originated factors against AIDCF controls under v1.0 §9 and §10, and baseline coders judge all factors against their methods.
- Reported: the distribution of factors by origin; every metric for all factors, for AIDCF-origin factors only and for baseline-origin factors only; and the number of factors merged or dropped, with reasons.

## 5. Paired classification and metric (KAI condition 3)

For each eligible factor and each method, and for BL-ANY, separately for strict readings (YES / DETECTED only) and inclusive readings (YES or PARTIAL / DETECTED or PARTIALLY_DETECTED):

| | Baseline surfaces | Baseline misses |
|---|---|---|
| AIDCF surfaces | Shared (a) | AIDCF-only (b) |
| AIDCF misses | Baseline-only (c) | Neither (d) |

- **Eligible paired factor (single method):** a consolidated factor that is knowable (STATED_KNOWN or DISCOVERABLE), identified before the reveal, with a determinate AIDCF judgement (DETECTED, PARTIALLY_DETECTED, NOT_DETECTED or NOT_ADDRESSED) and a determinate or OUT_OF_SCOPE judgement for the method. A pair is excluded if the method is NOT_APPLICABLE or INDETERMINATE, or if AIDCF is INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION. The number excluded is reported for each reason. An excluded pair never counts as a miss for either side.
- **BL-ANY rule (KAI further condition 1):** for each factor, take the methods that are not NOT_APPLICABLE. BL-ANY *surfaces* the factor if any of them is YES (strict) or YES or PARTIAL (inclusive). BL-ANY *misses* it only if every one of them is a determinate NO or OUT_OF_SCOPE. Otherwise (none surfaces it, and at least one is INDETERMINATE) BL-ANY is INDETERMINATE and the pair is excluded. If every method is NOT_APPLICABLE, BL-ANY is NOT_APPLICABLE and the pair is excluded. So AIDCF receives an AIDCF-only credit against BL-ANY only when its own judgement is determinate and every applicable method has been determinately judged not to surface the factor.
- **Net incremental coverage** = (b − c) / N, where N = a + b + c + d is the number of eligible paired factors for that method (or for BL-ANY) and that reading. It is always reported with a, b, c and d and the excluded counts shown. It ranges from −1 to +1, and a negative value is reported as plainly as a positive one.
- **Uncertainty:** factors are clustered within incidents, so 95% intervals use an incident-level cluster bootstrap (10,000 resamples, seed recorded in the run log). Not Wilson intervals, which assume independence.
- **Incident-level sensitivity:** the same table at incident level, where an incident counts as surfaced if any eligible factor is surfaced.
- No hypothesis test and no pass mark. The figures are descriptive.

## 6. Negative results stay visible (KAI condition 5)

- Every baseline-only pair (c) is listed by incident, factor and method in the main report, with the clause cited. It is a gap in AIDCF, recorded as G1 to G3 under §12.
- An exclusion may only use an `EXC-…` declared in `baselines.csv` before coding. Scope or exclusion arguments added after the results are seen are not allowed. Any later disagreement is reported as a disagreement, not resolved silently.

## 7. Limits of the claim (KAI condition 6; new BT-REQ)

**BT-REQ-0xx** Comparative results are exploratory. Every output that reports them states that a paper-based retrospective comparison does not show higher incident prevention, better predictive validity, lower operational risk, better cost-effectiveness, or better detection by real engineering teams. Those would need prospective or practitioner-based evaluation.

## 10. Open points for the reviewer

1. Is the baseline set right? In particular, should BL-ENG be split into power and cooling studies?
2. Should `operational_detectability` be required for every factor, or only for a sample, given the coding cost?
3. The minimum practitioner coder pool: one per method is proposed. Is that enough for the comparison to be credible?
